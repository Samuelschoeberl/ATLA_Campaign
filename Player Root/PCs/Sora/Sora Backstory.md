Character Concept: Firetribe Beastshaper

   Name: Sora  
Tribe Backstory
Long ago, the founders of my tribe forged deep spiritual bonds with twelve sacred animals, once believed to be the origin of the Chinese calendar. Through these bonds, they learned to shape fire into living embodiments of those animals. This art was never simple imitation: only by forming a genuine, lasting bond with a real animal species could a person breathe life into a fire-made creature.
Over generations, this practice narrowed. Most people can bond with only one animal, sometimes two. The idea that a single person could forge all twelve fire-animals has faded into myth, dismissed as exaggeration or madness. Some animals are now believed to be extinct altogether—and among them, dragons are considered the most fictional of all.
Dragons have not been seen in centuries. Many in the tribe insist they never existed, claiming they were symbolic creatures invented by the ancestors. Because of this, attempting to forge a fire-dragon is considered either heretical or foolish. The belief persists that dragons are impossible—not because they are forbidden, but because there is nothing left to bond with.
My grandfather was the lone exception. He claimed that the old stories were true and that dragons once walked the world as living beings. He was rumoured to have mastered far more fire-animals than should have been possible, though the village dismissed him as unstable. Just before his death at the age of 165, he revealed his greatest secret to me: he summoned a tiny fire-dragon, fragile but alive. In that moment, he proved that dragons were never myths. He whispered of a place beyond the sea where real dragons might still survive.
The tribe protects its island fiercely, clinging to isolation. They believe the outside world is cursed—ruled by chaos and death—and that leaving would invite destruction upon the island. Some whisper that this fear is tied to an ancient bargain made with the twelve beasts long ago, though no one remembers the terms.
No other tribe is known to create living fire-animals. Outsiders can shape fire into forms, but their creations are hollow—beautiful, but lifeless.


Personal Backstory

I was born on a remote island into a Firetribe that values control and restraint above all else.
Most people here forge one fire-animal, sometimes two. Anything beyond that is considered unrealistic. The old stories about masters who could forge all twelve are treated as folklore, and dragons are assumed to have never existed in the first place.
My grandfather didn’t agree. He believed the limits people accepted were based more on fear than truth. Near the end of his life, he showed me something he had kept hidden for decades: a small fire-dragon. It was unstable and weak, but it was alive. That was enough to convince me that the tribe was wrong.
Before he died, he told me that real dragons might still exist somewhere beyond the sea.
After that, staying on the island no longer made sense to me. I didn’t leave out of rebellion, and I didn’t tell anyone why I was going. I left because I wanted to understand how far this art could actually go.
I’ve started by forging fire-animals I already know well—the cat, the dog, and the rooster. Even those are difficult to maintain, and I lose control more often than I succeed. Still, I keep practicing. If forging a dragon is possible, then forging all twelve should be too, given enough time and experience.
Most people would say I’m wasting my life chasing something that doesn’t exist.
I think they’re just repeating what they were taught.
